Scientific Report: CoRoT-2 b

The 86-frame FITS dataset for CoRoT-2 b was processed with EXOTIC on the 2026-08-01 observing date. The observations were obtained with the Cecilia telescope at Whipple Observatory using a CCD camera with 2×2 binning and a Clear filter. FITS headers indicate an exposure interval of approximately 60–61 seconds, providing an approximately one-minute cadence. The observatory coordinates were reported as latitude +31.68° and longitude −110.88°, with an elevation of 1268 m.

EXOTIC performed plate solving, image alignment, aperture photometry, comparison-star testing, detrending, and transit-model fitting. The target coordinate was corrected using the successful astrometric solution from the initially supplied [142, 287] to [247, 211]. Several frames were reported as failing alignment, including a substantial group late in the sequence. EXOTIC also reported “Best Comparison Star: None,” indicating that the comparison-star ensemble did not provide a stable photometric reference for this dataset.

The fitted mid-transit time was 2461254.8427 ± 0.0037 BJD_TDB, and the fitted duration was 0.057 ± 0.024 days. However, the fit returned a radius ratio of 0.000 ± 0.055, corresponding to a formally negligible depth of (2.9 × 10⁻⁷ ± 5.9 × 10⁻⁴)%. This is inconsistent with the published CoRoT-2 b radius-ratio prior of approximately 0.1795 and indicates that the reduction did not detect a physically meaningful transit. The residual scatter was very large, 17.67%, and EXOTIC did not report a signal-to-noise ratio, reduced χ², or BIC.

Therefore, although EXOTIC completed successfully and generated output products, this reduction should not be interpreted as a reliable transit detection or planetary-parameter measurement. The fitted period remains a catalog prior rather than a new measurement. The likely limiting factors are unstable comparison-star photometry, frame-alignment failures, and/or an unsuitable comparison-star selection. The final light curve and diagnostic files should be treated as quality-control products and not as evidence confirming or refuting the known CoRoT-2 b transit.
