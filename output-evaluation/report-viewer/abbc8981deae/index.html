Data Reduction

The target star TRES-1 was identified at pixel coordinates [246, 175] via visual
identification. EXOTIC's plate solution refined this to [227, 201]. Four comparison
stars were specified: [338, 352], [333, 308], [95, 144], and [192, 150].
EXOTIC's optimization selected star #1 at [338, 352] as the best reference.
No flats or biases were available; dark frames existed in the dataset but were
not applied. Differential photometry with airmass detrending was performed.
Optimal aperture: 3.74 pixels; optimal annulus: 20.31 pixels.

Data Quality Assessment

  • Residual scatter: 22.72% (high, limiting precision)
  • Airmass range: 0.89 to 2.70
  • Airmass coefficients: a1 = 0.83 +/- 0.19, a2 = 0.064 +/- 0.051

The measured transit depth (3.38% +/- 1.87%) and radius ratio (0.184 +/- 0.051)
are consistent with published values (~2.18% depth, Rp/R* = 0.148) within
1-2 sigma. The large uncertainties reflect the high scatter typical of small-
aperture, unguided observations. Significant atmospheric extinction was detected
(a1 = 0.83), and the airmass correction was non-negligible.

This dataset demonstrates the feasibility of transit photometry with citizen-
science-scale instrumentation. Improved data quality would benefit from flat-
field calibration, guided tracking, and/or narrower-band filters.
