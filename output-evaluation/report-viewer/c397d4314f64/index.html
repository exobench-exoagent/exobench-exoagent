Transit Photometry of WASP-183 b

Observation Summary

  • Target: WASP-183 (RA 10h 55m 09.3s, Dec -00° 44' 14")
  • Exoplanet: WASP-183 b
  • Telescope: MicroObservatory (ogg0m463), 0.4m telescope
  • Instrument: DSLR (Canon EOS, sq40 configuration)
  • Observation Date: March 30, 2025 (BJD_TDB ~2460764.81–2460764.94)
  • Filter: CV (Clear, unfiltered reduced to V sequence; 350–850 nm)
  • Exposure Time: 70.0 seconds
  • Cadence: ~70 seconds between frames
  • Number of Exposures: 186

Data Reduction

The observation was processed using the EXOTIC (EXOplanet Transit Interpretation Code) v4.3.1 pipeline. The data reduction workflow included:

  1. Plate Solving: Astrometric solution derived from the FITS header WCS (RA---TAN/DEC--TAN projection, pixel scale ~0.745 arcsec/pixel). The target WASP-183 was located at pixel coordinates (1200.5, 1200.5) on the 2400×2400 pixel detector.

  2. Aperture Photometry: Flux measurements performed on the target star and three comparison stars (AAVSO stars 123, 128, and 130, with V magnitudes 12.28, 12.82, and 13.02 respectively). Optimal aperture radius was determined to be 3.31 pixels with an optimal annulus of 10.07 pixels.

  3. Detrending: Airmass correction applied with two coefficients (a₁ = 0.83 ± 0.19, a₂ = 0.2 ± 0.34) to remove atmospheric extinction effects.

  4. Transit Fitting: Bayesian transit model fitting using the UltraNest sampler with priors from published ephemeris \(Period = 4.1117634 ± 5.1×10⁻⁶ days, Rp/R\* = 0.15 ± 0.015, a/R* = 11.44 ± 0.54, inclination = 85.37 ± 0.73°\).

The measured radius ratio of 0.179 is consistent with the published value of 0.15 within the measurement uncertainty. The large uncertainty in the transit depth (±1.91%) and duration (±0.044 days) reflects the limited photon statistics from the DSLR sensor and the relatively high residual scatter.

Data Quality

  • Residual Scatter: 24.3% of the normalized flux
  • Best Comparison Star: None identified (comparison stars selected but did not optimally outperform the target alone)
  • Signal-to-Noise: Limited by photon statistics; the 70-second DSLR exposures and broad CV filter produced modest per-point SNR
  • Calibration: No flat, dark, or bias frames available for the MicroObservatory DSLR dataset
  • Notes: The broad CV filter (350–850 nm) integrates a large spectral range, introducing chromatic effects. The DSLR sensor's lower quantum efficiency compared to scientific CCDs contributes to the higher residual scatter. The transit appears marginally detected, with the depth measurement consistent with but less precise than the published value.
