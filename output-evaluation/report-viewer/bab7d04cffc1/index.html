Qatar-10 b Transit Observation Report

Observation Summary

The Qatar-10 b field was observed on 2024-07-15 with the Exoplanet Watch 0.4 m telescope system using a CCD camera. The dataset contains 140 FITS science images. The exposure time was 77 s, with an effective cadence of approximately 89 s based on consecutive BJD timestamps. The FITS header identifies an rp red-pass observation; EXOTIC reduced the data using a Johnson R-equivalent (RJ) limb-darkening band. The target was identified as Qatar-10 at RA 284.4439052° and Dec +69.5708373°.

Data Reduction

The observations were processed with EXOTIC v4.3.1. No dark, bias, or flat-field calibration frames were present, so the reduction proceeded without calibration-frame correction. Aperture photometry was performed using an optimal aperture radius of 3.0 pixels and an annulus radius of 6.5 pixels. Four comparison-star coordinates were supplied from the AAVSO finder-chart and telescope-image identification workflow. EXOTIC applied an airmass-based detrending correction and generated a normalized differential light curve. Limb-darkening parameters were calculated by EXOTIC using the Johnson R band.

Transit Results

The fitted mid-transit time is BJD_TDB 2460506.949 ± 0.010. The adopted orbital period is 1.64532652 ± 0.00000050 days. The fitted transit depth is 2.98 ± 1.72%, corresponding to a fitted radius ratio of 0.173 ± 0.050. The fitted transit duration is 0.113 ± 0.010 days (approximately 2.71 ± 0.24 hours). EXOTIC did not report a formal SNR, BIC, or reduced χ² value for this run.

Data Quality

EXOTIC completed successfully and produced the expected light-curve products. However, the residual scatter was 19.76%, which is very large for precision transit photometry. EXOTIC reported no best comparison star, suggesting that the comparison ensemble did not provide a stable reference at the achieved data quality. The transit-depth uncertainty is correspondingly substantial, and the fitted depth should be treated as a low-confidence detection or preliminary measurement rather than a precision refinement of the published ephemeris. No calibration frames were available, and the observation was unfiltered/red-pass rather than obtained through a tightly characterized standard photometric band.
