WASP-44 b Transit Photometry Report

Observation Summary

Target: WASP-44 b, a hot Jupiter transiting the G-type star WASP-44
Telescope: Cecilia (6-inch Maksutov-Newtonian, f/3.6, 560mm EFL)
Instrument: MicroObservatory automated telescope network (CfA/Smithsonian)
Observatory: Whipple Observatory, Amado, Arizona (31.68°N, 110.88°W, 1268m)
Observation Date: 2026-07-15
Filter: Clear (MObs CV passband: 350-850 nm)
Exposure Time: 60 seconds per frame
Detector: CCD (KAF-1402ME, 2x2 binning)
Pixel Scale: 5.0 arcsec/pixel
Image Size: 650 × 500 pixels
Frame Count: 37 science images (27 successfully aligned) + 4 dark frames
Cadence: ~180 seconds between exposures

Data Reduction

Calibration

  • Dark frames: 4 frames (60s each) used for dark subtraction. Dark frames matched in
    exposure time and temperature to science frames.

  • Flat-field: Not available. No flat field correction applied.

  • Bias: Not available. No bias correction applied.

Aperture Photometry

  • Optimal aperture radius: 2.46 pixels
  • Optimal annulus: 5.11 pixels
  • Comparison stars: 3 candidates analyzed; Star #3 at [464.0, 390.0] selected as best

Detrending

  • Method: Airmass correction using biweight detrending (LDTk)
  • Coefficients: a1 = 0.48 ± 0.21, a2 = 0.5227 ± 0.0024
  • Plate solving: Disabled (local 5.0 arcsec/pixel scale used)
  • Limb-darkening: LDTk model atmospheres with quadratic coefficients computed
    automatically from stellar parameters

Data Quality Notes

  • 9 of 36 frames failed image alignment (taken during telescope slewing)
  • 27 frames successfully registered and photometry extracted
  • 14 data points cover partial transit (early ingress phase)
  • Residual scatter: 44.46% (elevated due to limited coverage and atmospheric variability)

The measured Rp/R* of 0.156 is elevated compared to the published value of ~0.125. This
discrepancy is likely attributable to the high residual scatter (44.46%), limited transit
coverage (only early ingress observed), and potential inadequacies in the comparison star
selection. The narrow phase coverage (orbital phases -0.029 to -0.005) means the full
transit profile was not sampled, which can bias the derived parameters.

Signal-to-Noise and Fit Quality

The transit model was fit using nested sampling (Ultranest) with the following convergence:

  • dlogZ = 0.18 (converged, threshold: 0.5)
  • logZ error budget: single=0.26, bootstrap=0.18, total=0.18 (required <0.50)

The tight uncertainties on the fitted parameters (Tc: ±4.8×10⁻⁵, Rp/R*: ±1.1×10⁻⁵)
reflect the precision of the posterior distribution, though the accuracy is limited by
the systematic noise indicated by the 44.46% residual scatter.

Data Quality Assessment

Strengths:

  • Clean dark frame subtraction
  • Well-constrained posterior distributions from MCMC/nested sampling
  • Successful alignment of 27/36 frames
  • Optimal comparison star identification

Limitations:

  • High residual scatter (44.46%) indicates significant uncorrected systematics
  • Limited transit coverage (only early ingress, ~90 minutes)
  • Only 14 data points during/around transit
  • No flat-field or bias calibration
  • 25% data loss due to failed alignment

Recommendations:

  • Additional observations covering more of the transit profile would significantly
    improve parameter accuracy

  • Flat-field calibration would reduce systematic noise

  • Longer integration times or binning would improve SNR
  • Observations at multiple orbital phases would enable better detrending
