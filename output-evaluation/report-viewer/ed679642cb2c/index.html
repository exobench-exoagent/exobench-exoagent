Scientific Report: Transit Observation of HAT-P-36 b

Observation Summary

The exoplanet transit of HAT-P-36 b was observed on March 19, 2026, using the MicroObservatory 0.4m telescope. The data were acquired using a CCD camera with an SDSS-rp (Sloan r) filter. The target star, HAT-P-36, was centered in the field of view, and the observations were conducted at a site elevation of 2042.08 meters (Latitude: 30.679967, Longitude: -104.015036).

Data Reduction and Processing

Data reduction was performed using the EXOTIC (EXOplanet Transit Interpretation Code) pipeline. The following steps were taken:

  1. Calibration & Pre-processing: FITS images were processed using aperture photometry. Calibration frames (Flats, Darks, Biases) were not provided and thus omitted from the reduction.
  2. Star Identification: The target star was identified via a plate solve, resulting in pixel coordinates of [1175, 1181].
  3. Photometry: Optimal aperture (6.21) and annulus (12.36) sizes were determined to maximize the signal-to-noise ratio.
  4. Detrending: The light curve was corrected for airmass effects using a second-order polynomial fit (Airmass coefficients: 9.28 and -2.12).
  5. Transit Fitting: A Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) fit was performed to determine the planetary parameters, with limb-darkening coefficients calculated for the Sloan r filter.

Transit Results

The transit fitting yielded the following results:

  • Mid-Transit Time: 2461118.8009 $\pm$ 0.0055 BJD_TDB
  • Transit Depth: 2.48 $\pm$ 0.24 %
  • Radius Ratio ($R_p/R_s$): 0.1574 $\pm$ 0.0077
  • Transit Duration: 0.0995 $\pm$ 0.0019 days
  • Orbital Inclination: 89.0 $\pm$ 2.22$^\circ$
  • Residual Scatter: 4.2 %

Data Quality and Analysis

The fit quality was high, with a residual scatter of 4.2%. The planetary parameters are consistent with the expected values for HAT-P-36 b. The plate solve successfully resolved the target coordinates, and the airmass correction effectively removed systemic trends from the light curve.
