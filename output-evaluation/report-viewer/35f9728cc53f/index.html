KELT-7 b Transit Observation Report

Observation Summary

The target was KELT-7, observed on 2017-01-07 with the Exoplanet Watch 0.4-m telescope system, instrument kb27/0m4-06, using a CCD camera. The FITS headers identify an ip filter, while the reduction was treated as clear/unfiltered photometry for limb-darkening purposes. The dataset contains 130 science images. Individual exposures were approximately 3.086 seconds, with a median cadence of approximately 23 seconds based on the final time series.

Data Reduction

EXOTIC processed the science images using aperture photometry with an optimized aperture radius of 6.89 pixels and an annulus radius of 10.17 pixels. No dark, bias, or flat calibration frames were present; these were intentionally left absent rather than substituting the science-image directory. Four comparison stars were supplied, with Comparison 2 at pixel coordinates [956, 433] selected by EXOTIC as the best comparison star. Image alignment and astrometric plate solving completed successfully using an Astrometry.net WCS solution. The light curve was normalized and modeled with airmass detrending and a transit model. Limb-darkening parameters were calculated with uncertainties using a customized 350–850 nm passband appropriate for clear photometry.

Transit Results

The fitted mid-transit time is BJD_TDB 2457758.16271 ± 0.00097. The adopted orbital period is 2.7347656 ± 0.0000002 days. EXOTIC measured a transit depth of 0.87 ± 0.62%, corresponding to a radius ratio of Rp/R* = 0.093 ± 0.033. The fitted transit duration is 0.009 ± 0.010 days, or approximately 13 ± 14 minutes. A numerical SNR, BIC, and reduced χ² were not reported in the generated EXOTIC summary.

Data Quality

The reduction completed successfully with return code 0, and all processed frames were flagged as usable by the plate-status output. However, the reported residual scatter is approximately 20.04%, substantially larger than the fitted transit depth. The large uncertainty on the depth and duration indicates that this observation provides weak evidence for a precise transit measurement. Results should therefore be considered low-confidence and are not suitable for refining the KELT-7 b ephemeris without additional observations.
