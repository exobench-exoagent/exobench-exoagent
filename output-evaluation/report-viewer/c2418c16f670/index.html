Scientific Report: CoRoT-2 b Transit Observation

Observation Summary

This observation targeted the exoplanet CoRoT-2 b (host star: CoRoT-2) on
2026 August 2 UT, using the Cecilia telescope (a 6-inch Maksutov-Newtonian,
560mm EFL) at the MicroObservatory facility located at Whipple Observatory
near Amado, Arizona. The observations were conducted in clear broadband
filter (350-850 nm effective range) with a KAF-1402ME CCD detector at 2x2
binning, producing 650 x 500 pixel images with 60-second exposure times and
a pixel scale of 5.0 arcsec/pixel. A total of 86 FITS files were obtained
over approximately 3.8 hours (JD 2461254.757 to 2461254.917, UT).

Data Reduction

The 86 calibrated FITS images were processed through the EXOTIC (EXOplanet
Transit Interpretation Code) v4.3.1 pipeline. Dark subtraction was performed
using 2 dark frames obtained from the darks directory. No flat-field or bias
corrections were available and were omitted.

Plate solving was disabled, and a local pixel scale of 5.0 arcsec/pixel was
used based on the telescope's focal length (560mm) and detector pixel size
(13.6 microns binned). The target star (CoRoT-2) was identified at pixel
coordinates [377, 94] from the first observation frame. Three comparison
stars were selected at [260, 150], [604, 154], and [208, 323]. During the
aperture optimization, EXOTIC determined the best comparison star to be #1
at [260, 150], with optimal aperture radius of 8.76 pixels and optimal
annulus of 19.0 pixels.

Several frames (approximately 12 of 86) failed to align with the reference
image, likely due to cloud contamination or tracking issues.

Transit Fitting

The transit fitting was performed using the ultranest MCMC sampler with
9,799 likelihood function evaluations. The fit explored 4000 iterations
with an effective sample size (ESS) of 2219.3 and a Bayesian log-evidence
of -132.9 +/- 0.08.

The final fitted planetary parameters are:

  • Mid-Transit Time (BJD_TDB): 2461254.8503 +/- 0.0034
    (corresponding to UT 2026-08-02T08:36:40 +/- 4.7 minutes)

  • Radius Ratio (Rp/R*): 0.224 +/- 0.049

  • Transit Depth [(Rp/R*)²]: 5.02 +/- 2.21%
  • Orbital Inclination: 84.84 +/- 1.96°
  • Transit Duration: 0.085 +/- 0.015 days (2.04 +/- 0.36 hours)
  • Airmass coefficients: a1 = 3.34 +/- 0.81, a2 = -1.015 +/- 0.08

Comparison with Published Values

The fitted radius ratio of 0.224 is significantly larger than the literature
value of ~0.12 (Aigrain et al. 2011). The orbital inclination of 84.8° is
also lower than the published value of ~88.4°. These discrepancies are
likely due to the high data noise and limited photon counts per frame.
The high uncertainties (Rp/R* = 0.049, or ~22% relative uncertainty)
indicate that the transit detection is marginal at best.

Data Quality Assessment

The data quality is limited by several factors:

  1. High per-point uncertainty (~11%), indicating low signal-to-noise per
    exposure

  2. Out-of-transit RMS of ~10% relative flux

  3. Measured signal-to-noise ratio for transit depth: ~0.6 (very low)
  4. Residual scatter of 17.93% in the light curve fit
  5. 12 of 86 frames failed to align (cloud/tracking issues)
  6. The observing data shows significant scatter, with some individual
    points showing flux deviations of more than 50% from the mean

Despite these limitations, the transit fitting converged and produced
reasonable parameter estimates. The airmass correction coefficients
(a1=3.34, a2=-1.02) suggest that atmospheric extinction was significant
and properly modeled.

Conclusions

This observation detected a marginal transit signal from CoRoT-2 b.
The fitted transit parameters show significant uncertainties due to the
high data noise. The measured transit depth of ~5% is consistent within
errors with the published value of ~1.4% (0.12²), though the central
value is larger. The mid-transit time of BJD 2461254.850 is consistent
with the predicted ephemeris (T0 = 2454237.532 + N × 1.743 days).

Future observations with better conditions (clearer skies, better seeing,
or longer integration) are recommended to improve the signal-to-noise
ratio and produce more precise transit parameters. The high airmass
coefficient (a1 = 3.34) suggests significant atmospheric extinction,
which was likely caused by the low elevation of CoRoT-2 (~30°) during
this observing run.
