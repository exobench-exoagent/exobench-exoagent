WASP-43 b Transit Observation Report

Observation Summary

The target was WASP-43, observed on 2026-03-12 with the Exoplanet Watch 0.4-meter telescope using a CCD camera. The data consist of 179 FITS science images obtained through an LCO Sloan r filter (SR). Each exposure was 60 seconds, with an approximately 63-second cadence based on the recorded time series. The observation covered approximately 0.13 days.

Data Reduction

The FITS images were processed with EXOTIC v4.3.1. No dark, bias, or flat-field calibration frames were present, so the reduction proceeded without those calibration products. Astrometry.net provided a successful plate solution, and the target coordinates were verified against the WASP-43 catalog position. Aperture photometry was performed with an optimal aperture radius of 7.66 pixels and an annulus radius of 7.97 pixels. The resulting differential light curve was detrended using airmass-dependent correction terms and fitted with a transit model. Limb-darkening coefficients were calculated using LDTk for the Sloan r passband.

Transit Results

The fitted mid-transit time is BJD_TDB = 2461111.73939 ± 0.00087. The adopted orbital period prior is 0.81347407 ± 0.00000002 days. The fitted transit depth is 1.91 ± 0.20%, corresponding to a fitted radius ratio of 0.1383 ± 0.0072. The transit duration is 0.0479 ± 0.0019 days, or approximately 1.15 ± 0.05 hours. The depth-to-fit-uncertainty ratio gives an estimated parameter SNR of approximately 9.6. The fitted inclination is 81.55 ± 0.51 degrees.

Data Quality

The final residual scatter reported by EXOTIC is 0.76%. The fit completed successfully, with EXOTIC reporting acceptable nested-sampling convergence and a final model quality of “ok.” No reduced χ² or BIC values were reported in the generated EXOTIC products. The residual scatter is substantial relative to the transit depth, so the transit detection should be interpreted as useful but not highly precise. EXOTIC did not identify a single best comparison star; the light curve therefore includes the limitations associated with the selected field-star ensemble and the absence of calibration frames.
