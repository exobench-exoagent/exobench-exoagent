Scientific Report: Exoplanet Transit Observation of KELT-7 b

Observation Summary

The exoplanet transit of KELT-7 b was observed using the MicroObservatory telescope with a CCD camera. The observation took place on January 6, 2017. The data was acquired using an Open filter (reduced to the CV sequence), which is a clear unfiltered band typical of MicroObservatory observations. The dataset consists of 130 FITS images.

Data Reduction

The data reduction was performed using the EXOTIC (EXOplanet Transit Interpretation Code) pipeline.

  • Calibration: No calibration frames (darks, biases, flats) were provided; the pipeline proceeded with science frames only.
  • Aperture Photometry: An optimal aperture of 3.03 pixels and an annulus of 7.57 pixels were determined to minimize noise.
  • Star Identification: The target star KELT-7 was identified at pixel coordinates [736, 543] via a plate solution. Comparison stars were automatically selected and integrated from the AAVSO catalog to provide a stable relative flux reference.
  • Transit Fitting: A Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach was used to fit the transit light curve, determining the most probable planetary parameters.

Transit Results

The analysis yielded the following planetary parameters:

  • Mid-Transit Time: $2457758.16479 \pm 0.00096$ BJD_TDB
  • Transit Depth: $1.44\% \pm 0.82\%$
  • Transit Duration: $0.027 \pm 0.0057$ days
  • Radius Ratio ($R_p/R_s$): $0.12 \pm 0.034$
  • Orbital Inclination: $88.76^\circ \pm 2.91^\circ$

Data Quality

The fit shows a residual scatter of 18.67%, indicating a relatively noisy observation. The transit depth uncertainty is significant, which is common for small-aperture observations of this target. The calculated orbital inclination is consistent with the expected near-edge-on geometry of the KELT-7 system.
