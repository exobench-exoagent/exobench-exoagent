WASP-183 b Transit Observation Report

Observation Summary

The target was WASP-183, observed on 2025-03-30 UTC with the Exoplanet Watch 0.4-meter telescope at Haleakala Observatory. The instrument was a CCD camera operated at 1×1 pixel binning. The FITS headers identify the observing filter as SDSS-rp (rp, LCO SDSS-RP-117), with 70-second exposures. The median cadence was approximately 73 seconds, based on the timestamps in the final light-curve time series. A total of 173 science FITS images were processed.

Data Reduction

The EXOTIC Standard notebook workflow was used to load and inspect the image sequence, identify the target and comparison stars, generate a complete inits.json↗, and perform the reduction. No dark, bias, or flat-field calibration directories were present; these were intentionally left null rather than substituting the science-image directory. The target and four comparison stars were selected using the FITS source catalog and positional matching to the published WASP-183 coordinates. EXOTIC selected comparison star 1, at pixel position [1190.33, 1105.95], as the best comparison star.

EXOTIC performed PSF photometry, differential normalization, airmass detrending, and transit-model fitting. Limb-darkening coefficients were calculated by EXOTIC using the standard clear/unfiltered CV bandpass approximation with a 350–850 nm wavelength range.

Transit Results

The final fitted mid-transit time is BJD_TDB 2460764.8803 ± 0.0026. The adopted orbital period is 4.1117634 ± 0.0000051 days, from the planetary-parameter database. The fitted transit depth is 3.18 ± 0.53%, corresponding to a radius ratio of Rp/R* = 0.178 ± 0.015. The fitted transit duration is 0.1019 ± 0.0057 days, or approximately 2.45 ± 0.14 hours. The approximate depth-to-uncertainty signal-to-noise ratio is 6.0.

Data Quality

The residual scatter reported by EXOTIC is 1.75% RMS. All processed frames were reported as in-frame and free of FITS, alignment, low-flux, and sky-background warnings. The transit fit completed successfully with a satisfactory UltraNest convergence assessment. Reduced chi-squared and BIC were not included in the EXOTIC final-parameter output. The relatively large residual scatter and broad transit-depth uncertainty indicate that this is a useful transit detection but not a high-precision parameter determination.
