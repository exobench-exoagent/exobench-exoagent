Scientific Report: Transit Observation of KELT-9 b

Observation Summary

The transit of the ultra-hot Jupiter KELT-9 b was observed using the MicroObservatory telescope. The dataset consists of 300 FITS images acquired on 2023-09-24. Observations were performed using a CCD camera with a Sloan i (SI) filter, with an exposure time of 40 seconds and a binned pixel scale of 2x2. The nominal pixel scale of the system is 0.778 arcsec/pixel.

Data Reduction

The data were processed using the EXOTIC (EXOplanet Transit Interpretation Code) pipeline. Initial visual identification provided coordinates for the target and four comparison stars. Due to a segmentation fault encountered during the plate-solving phase of the initial run, plate solving was disabled in the final successful reduction, relying instead on the known instrument pixel scale.

Aperture photometry was performed using an optimal aperture of 28.36 pixels and an annulus of 89.81 pixels. Limb-darkening parameters were calculated by EXOTIC for the Sloan i filter. The resulting light curve was detrended and fitted using a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach via the UltraNest sampler.

Transit Results

The fitting process yielded the following parameters:

  • Mid-Transit Time: 2460211.9633 $\pm$ 0.0034 BJD_TDB
  • Radius Ratio ($R_p/R_*$): 0.029 $\pm$ 0.03
  • Transit Depth: 0.087 $\pm$ 0.18 %
  • Orbital Inclination: 81.89 $\pm$ 2.39$^\circ$
  • Transit Duration: 0.026 $\pm$ 0.061 days
  • Residual Scatter: 0.28 %

Data Quality and Discussion

The reduction successfully converged with a residual scatter of 0.28%. However, the fitted radius ratio (0.029) is considerably smaller than the published values for KELT-9 b, and the uncertainty in the transit depth is relatively high. EXOTIC reported "Best Comparison Star: None", suggesting that none of the selected comparison stars provided a perfectly stable reference, which likely contributed to the increased uncertainty and the deviation of the radius ratio from catalog values.
