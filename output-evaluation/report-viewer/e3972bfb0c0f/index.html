Scientific Report: Transit Observation of CoRoT-2 b

Observation Summary

The target exoplanet CoRoT-2 b was observed using the Cecilia telescope at the Whipple Observatory (Center for Astrophysics | Harvard-Smithsonian). Observations were conducted on 2026-08-01 using a CCD camera with a 2x2 pixel binning configuration and a Clear filter. The telescope's image scale was verified at 5.0 arcsec/pixel. The dataset consists of 86 FITS frames with an exposure time of 60 seconds per frame.

Data Reduction

The data were processed using the EXOTIC transit pipeline. The reduction included the following steps:

  1. Star Identification: The target star CoRoT-2 was identified at pixel coordinates [247, 211] via plate solving. Four comparison stars were utilized, with star #3 at [550, 410] providing the best stability.
  2. Aperture Photometry: Optimal aperture and annulus sizes were determined to be 4.04 and 9.03 pixels, respectively.
  3. Detrending and Fitting: The pipeline performed a Bayesian fit to the light curve, accounting for airmass coefficients to correct for atmospheric extinction. The airmass coefficients were found to be 2.04 +/- 0.43 and -0.55 +/- 0.12.

Transit Results

The transit fit yielded the following planetary parameters:

  • Mid-Transit Time: 2461254.838 +/- 0.0036 BJD_TDB
  • Transit Depth: 4.43 +/- 2.65 %
  • Radius Ratio (Rp/Rs): 0.211 +/- 0.063
  • Transit Duration: 0.069 +/- 0.025 days
  • Orbital Inclination: 83.06 +/- 2.08 degrees

Data Quality

The fit produced a residual scatter of 17.74%, indicating significant noise in the observed light curve. While the mid-transit time is well-constrained, the uncertainties in transit depth and duration are relatively high, likely due to the signal-to-noise ratio of the dataset. The Bayesian evidence (logZ = -22.81) and posterior distributions indicate a successful, albeit noisy, detection of the transit event.
