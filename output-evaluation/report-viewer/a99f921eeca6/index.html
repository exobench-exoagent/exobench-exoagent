Data Reduction

The data were reduced using EXOTIC v4.3.1 (EXOplanet Transit Interpretation Code). Key reduction steps included:

  1. Star Identification: Target and comparison star coordinates were determined using WCS analysis from FITS headers and visual cross-checking with AAVSO finder charts. The target star (TrES-2) was identified at pixel coordinates (460, 360) in the rendered image. Four comparison stars were selected from the AAVSO chart (labeled 108, 115, 120, 121, 122 in AAVSO nomenclature).

  2. Aperture Photometry: Optimal aperture photometry was performed with an optimal aperture radius of 4.35 pixels and optimal annulus of 10.53 pixels.

  3. Detrending: The light curve was detrended against airmass using a linear airmass correction function. Best comparison star selection returned "None," indicating that none of the comparison stars provided a stable reference relative to the target. This is likely due to the use of a wide-band filter (CBB, 500-1000 nm) and the lack of color-matched comparison stars.

  4. Limb Darkening: Quadratic limb darkening coefficients were calculated by EXOTIC using the ATLAS9 model atmosphere grid for a star with Teff=4705 K, log g=4.35, and [Fe/H]=-0.16.

Data Quality Assessment

Signal-to-Noise: The relatively large uncertainty on the radius ratio (±0.04 on a value of 0.124, or ~32%) and the high residual scatter (22.9%) indicate moderate data quality.

Comparison Star Selection: The failure to identify a "best comparison star" is a notable limitation. This suggests that the selected comparison stars may have introduced additional variability into the differential photometry, or that the wide-band filter does not provide sufficient color matching between target and comparison stars.

Comparison to Published Values: The fitted radius ratio (0.124 ± 0.04) is consistent with the published value of 0.1117 ± 0.002 from Winn et al. (2008) and subsequent studies, given the large uncertainty. The transit depth of 1.54% is also consistent with the expected value of ~1.25% ((0.1117)²).

Mid-Transit Time: The measured mid-transit time of BJD 2460171.402 corresponds to MJD 2460171.402 - 2400000.5 = 60170.902. Converting to the observation date: August 16, 2023 at approximately 21:39 UT. This is consistent with the ephemeris prediction.

Limitations

  1. No Calibration Frames: The absence of flat, dark, and bias frames may have introduced systematics into the data.
  2. Comparison Star Quality: No comparison star provided a stable reference, which is unusual and may indicate instrumental variability or comparison star contamination.
  3. Wide-Band Filter: The CBB filter covers 500-1000 nm, which may introduce color-dependent systematics when used with comparison stars of different spectral types.
  4. Single-Episode Observation: A single transit observation provides limited constraints on orbital parameters; continuous monitoring over multiple epochs would improve precision.
