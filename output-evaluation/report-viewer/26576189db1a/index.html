  • Observing Notes: No calibration frames (darks, flats, biases) were available with this dataset. Plate solution was requested and executed automatically.

Data Reduction

The reduction was performed using EXOTIC v4.3.1 (EXOplanet Transit Interpretation Code). The pipeline executed the following steps:

  1. Plate Solving: Automatic ASTAP-based plate solving was performed on all 179 FITS files to determine celestial coordinates.
  2. Photometry: Aperture photometry was performed on the target star and four comparison stars. The optimal aperture radius was 6.71 pixels with an optimal annulus of 7.97 pixels.
  3. Differential Photometry: The target flux was normalized against the comparison star combination.
  4. Detrending: Airmass detrending was applied using a quadratic airmass correction function with coefficients a1 = 1.111 ± 0.012 and a2 = -0.0846 ± 0.005.
  5. Limb Darkening: Non-linear limb darkening coefficients were calculated using LDTk based on stellar parameters (Teff ≈ 4800 K, log g ≈ 4.33, [Fe/H] ≈ -0.02).
  6. Transit Fitting: A Bayesian transit model was fit using UltrANest (MCMC sampler) with priors from the NASA Exoplanet Archive.

Data Quality

Fit Quality: The MCMC analysis (23,802 likelihood evaluations, 1,567 effective samples) converged successfully. The evidence uncertainty (dlogZ = 0.09) satisfied the < 0.50 threshold.

Residuals: The residual scatter in the light curve fit is 1.04%. The overall RMS of the normalized flux is 1.68%, which is higher than expected for a typical high-quality transit observation. This may be due to:

  • Higher-than-expected photon noise given the target magnitude (~11.9 mag) and 60-second exposures
  • Atmospheric variability not fully corrected by the airmass detrending
  • No flat-field calibration available

Caveats:

  • No calibration frames were used, which may introduce systematic errors
  • The fitted transit depth (0.1 ± 0.089%) is consistent with zero within ~1σ, suggesting the data quality may not be sufficient to conclusively detect the transit
  • The literature Rp/R* ratio for WASP-43 b is ~0.1086 (Hellier et al. 2012), which is significantly larger than our fitted value of 0.032 ± 0.014
  • The large uncertainty on Rp/R* suggests the data may not contain a well-sampled transit, or the photometric precision is insufficient

Warnings: EXOTIC reported that "Best Comparison Star: None" was selected, indicating that the comparison star combination did not significantly improve the photometry over the raw target flux.
