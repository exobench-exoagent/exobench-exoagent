Data Reduction

Calibration frames (biases, darks, flats) were not available for this dataset. EXOTIC performed plate solving using the ASTROMETRY.NET service, successfully identifying 121 standard filter files for limb darkening table lookups via LDTk. Aperture photometry was performed with optimal aperture radius of 5.29 pixels and annulus of 8.2 pixels. The detrending was performed using airmass correction with two coefficients (a1 = 0.073 ± 0.027, a2 = 1.72 ± 0.70). Five comparison stars were selected from the AAVSO chart, though EXOTIC reported no single best comparison star.

Data Quality

Fit Quality: The transit fit exhibits very large uncertainties. The radius ratio Rp/R* = 0.018 ± 0.037 is consistent with zero, indicating no statistically significant transit detection. The transit depth measurement (0.031 ± 0.13%) is also consistent with zero flux variation.

Residual Behavior: The residual scatter is 39.49%, which is very high for transit photometry. This suggests significant systematic noise in the light curve, likely due to:

  • The observation being made with an unfiltered (clear) camera, resulting in a broad bandpass and potentially variable atmospheric transmission
  • Lack of suitable comparison stars that share the same seeing conditions
  • The target star being at or near the corner of the field of view (coordinates [2399, 2399] on a 2400×2400 detector)

Uncertainties: The large uncertainties on all parameters (especially the transit depth and duration) indicate this observation does not provide a robust measurement of the transit. The mid-transit time uncertainty of 0.52 BJD (~12 hours) is much larger than the expected transit duration.

Conclusion: This observation of Qatar-10 b did not yield a statistically significant transit detection. The high residual scatter (39.49%) and poor photometric stability suggest that follow-up observations with better conditions, a more stable comparison star, or differential photometry with multiple comparison stars would be needed to confirm the transit signal. The published parameters for Qatar-10 b (Bakos et al. 2015: period 3.619429 days, Rp/R* ~0.1) are not well-reproduced by this dataset.
