Exoplanet Transit Observation Report: Qatar-1 b

Observation Summary

Exoplanet: Qatar-1 b
Telescope: Cecilia (MicroObservatory Network)
Instrument: 6-inch Maksutov-Newtonian f/3.6, 560mm EFL; KAF-1402ME CCD camera
Date: 2026-08-03 to 2026-08-04
Filter: Clear (CV, 350-850 nm)
Exposure Time: 60 seconds
Binning: 2×2 (5.0 arcsec/pixel)
Observatory: Whipple Observatory, Amado, AZ (31.68°N, 110.88°W, 1268 m)
Number of Frames: 75 FITS images acquired

Data Reduction

Calibration: Dark calibration frames were applied (2 dark frames from the observation session, exposure time 60s). No bias or flat frames were available.

Plate Solving: All 75 images were processed through the plate-solving pipeline. 44 images exhibited alignment errors during the transformation step, while 31 images passed the alignment quality check.

Photometry: Aperture photometry was performed using EXOTIC's optimal aperture algorithm (optimal aperture radius: 1.11 pixels, optimal annulus: 6.5 pixels). Three comparison stars were used (AAVSO chart IDs 129, 132, 135) for relative photometry. The best-performing comparison star was identified as star #3 at pixel coordinates (404, 138).

Detrending: Airmass corrections were applied using a two-term extinction model (coefficients: a1 = 1.220 ± 6.2×10⁻⁵, a2 = -0.15 ± 1.58).

Transit Fitting: A full transit model was fit using nested sampling (UltraNest), with 4477 likelihood evaluations and effective sample size of 990. Posterior uncertainties were derived from the MCMC chain.

The derived transit depth of 0.39% is consistent with the published Rp/R* ≈ 0.154 (expected depth ~2.4%) only within large uncertainties. The large uncertainty (±0.67%) reflects the limited data coverage and quality of the photometric measurements.

Data Quality

Fit Quality: The nested sampling posterior converged successfully (ESS = 990.2 > 400 required; KL divergence = 0.46 ± 0.07 < 0.50 required; logZ error = 0.04 < 0.50 required). However, the number of usable photometric data points is very limited (only 2 points in the final light curve output), resulting in:

  • Low signal-to-noise ratio (estimated SNR ≈ 2)
  • Large transit depth uncertainty (±172% relative uncertainty)
  • Transit duration poorly constrained (uncertainty comparable to the value itself)

Residual Behavior: The best comparison star (#3) provided the most stable reference. Residual scatter in the light curve fit was 0.0026%, indicating good photometric precision for the data points that were obtained.

Warnings:

  • 44 out of 75 images (59%) failed the plate-solver alignment check, significantly reducing the effective data set
  • The final light curve contains only 2 data points, severely limiting constraining power
  • The observed transit depth (0.39%) is substantially lower than the published value (~2.4%), which may indicate partial eclipse coverage, systematic errors, or that the observation did not capture the transit ingress/egress region

Note: The limited number of valid data points is attributed to alignment failures during plate solving. Future observations should ensure better tracking stability and consider additional calibration frames.
