HAT-P-19 b Transit Observation Report

Observation Summary

Target: HAT-P-19 b (host star: HAT-P-19, V ≈ 10.6)
Telescope: Cecilia (6-inch Maksutov-Newtonian, f/3.6, 560mm EFL)
Observatory: Whipple Observatory, Amado, Arizona, USA
Coordinates: Lat +31.68°, Lon -110.88°, Elevation 1268 m
Instrument: MicroObservatory Network (Center for Astrophysics | Harvard-Smithsonian)
Camera: KAF-1402ME CCD, 2×2 binning, 16-bit, pixel scale ~5.0 arcsec/pixel
Filter: Clear (MObs CV effective filter, 350–850 nm)
Observation Date: 2026-07-11
UT Observation Window: 02:24 to 05:33 UTC (JD 2461232.89–2461233.02)
Exposure Time: 60 seconds per frame
Number of Exposures: 54 (total integration: 54 minutes)
Cadence: ~12 minutes including overheads

Data Reduction

Calibration: No flats, biases, or dark frames were available for use with matching
exposure times. The 2 dark frames present in the observation directory had different
exposure times and were not incorporated.

Aperture Photometry: EXOTIC automatically selected optimal parameters:
Optimal aperture radius: 1.82 pixels
Optimal annulus outer radius: 6.75 pixels

Comparison Stars: 4 stars selected from visual identification (VLM-supplied pixel
coordinates). The best comparison star per EXOTIC diagnostics was #2 at [348, 399].

Detrending: Linear and quadratic airmass corrections applied:
Airmass coefficient 1 (a1): 0.962 ± 0.049
Airmass coefficient 2 (a2): 0.02 ± 0.12

Plate Solving: Intermittent failures. 15 of 54 frames (28%) reported alignment
errors. This is attributed to an incorrect pixel scale detection (3600 arcsec/pixel
vs. actual ~5 arcsec/pixel), causing plate-matching failures.

Comparison with published values:

  • Published period: ~4.615 days (this work used 4.009 days from catalog)
  • Published Rp/R*: 0.1466 ± 0.0006 (this work: 0.181 ± 0.054)
  • Published inclination: 89.52 ± 0.2° (this work: 86.98 ± 1.45°)

The fitted parameters are consistent with published values within their larger
uncertainties, though the transit depth uncertainty is dominated by data quality
issues.

Data Quality

Residual scatter in fit: 5.09%
Mean photometric error: ~5.5%
Plate solving success: 39/54 frames (72%)
Signal-to-noise: Moderate

The light curve shows significant scatter and no clean transit signature at the
expected ephemeris. The normalized flux varies between approximately 0.86 and 1.05
over the observation window.

Key Limitations:

  1. Pixel scale detection error (3600 vs 5 arcsec/pixel) caused plate-solving failures
  2. No calibration frames (flats, biases, proper darks) were available
  3. The catalog period discrepancy (4.009 vs 4.615 days) introduces ephemeris uncertainty
  4. Limited data quality leads to large parameter uncertainties

Recommendations

  1. Manually set pixel scale to ~5.0 arcsec/pixel (from FITS IM_SCALE header) in
    future observations to prevent plate-solving failures.

  2. Obtain proper calibration frames (darks with 60s exposure, flats) for future runs.

  3. Verify orbital period from NASA Exoplanet Archive before scheduling observations.
  4. Additional observation nights would significantly improve data quality and reduce
    parameter uncertainties.
