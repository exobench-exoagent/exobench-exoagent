TrES-2 b Transit Observation Report

Observation Summary

The TrES-2 b observation was obtained on 2023-08-17 UTC with the Exoplanet Watch 0.4-meter telescope using a CCD camera. The dataset contains 300 science FITS images. The exposure time was 6.524 s, with an approximately 10 s cadence inferred from the timestamps. Images were acquired through an Astrodon ExoPlanet-BB clear blue-blocking filter, represented in EXOTIC as filter CBB with an adopted 500–999.9 nm bandpass. The target was identified as TrES-2 at approximately RA 19:07:14.046, Dec +49:18:59.116 (J2000), using the FITS WCS solution, AAVSO finder chart, and visual matching.

Data Reduction

EXOTIC v4.3.1 performed the reduction. No dark, bias, or flat frames were present, so the reduction proceeded without calibration-frame correction. Plate-solved coordinates were used for the target and four AAVSO-labelled comparison stars. Aperture photometry was performed with an optimized aperture radius of 8.02 pixels and an annulus radius of 60.94 pixels. The resulting differential light curve was detrended using airmass terms and fitted with EXOTIC’s transit model. EXOTIC calculated limb-darkening information using the CBB bandpass approximation.

Transit Results

The fitted mid-transit time is BJD_TDB 2460173.6971 ± 0.00097. The adopted orbital period is 2.470613372 ± 0.000000024 days, based on the planetary ephemeris prior. The fitted transit depth is 0.84 ± 0.67%, corresponding to a radius ratio of Rp/R* = 0.092 ± 0.036. The fitted transit duration is 0.0288 ± 0.0091 days, or approximately 41.5 ± 13.1 minutes.

The formal depth-to-uncertainty signal-to-noise estimate is approximately 1.25, indicating that the transit signal is weak in this single observation. EXOTIC reports a residual scatter of 0.69% RMS. BIC and reduced χ² were not reported in the generated EXOTIC products.

Data Quality

EXOTIC completed successfully and produced the final light curve and diagnostic products. The posterior fit was reported as acceptable by the nested-sampling run, but the large fractional uncertainty on the transit depth and the approximately 1.25σ depth significance make this observation unsuitable as a standalone confirmation. EXOTIC did not identify a single preferred comparison star, suggesting that comparison-star combination or field-systematics limitations may be affecting the precision. The absence of calibration frames and the use of a broad clear blue-blocking bandpass should also be considered when interpreting the result.
