Data Quality Assessment

Light Curve Characteristics:

  • The normalized flux shows significant scatter (~1-3% per point) which is typical for 0.4m telescope observations of a ~12-13 mag star with 20-second exposures.
  • Several data points show flux values exceeding 1.02 and below 0.96, suggesting the presence of systematic variations, potential cloud/transient features, or outlier frames.
  • The residual scatter of 1.12% after detrending is reasonable for this instrument and star brightness.

Comparison Star Performance:

  • EXOTIC selected comparison star #2 at [812, 888] as the best reference.
  • The airmass coefficients (a1=0.929, a2=0.0606) suggest the detrending model captured the systematic trends reasonably well.

Limitations:

  • The transit duration came out as 0.0 ± 0.0 days, indicating the data coverage did not span a full transit. The observation likely captured only the egress or ingress, or a portion of the flat-bottomed transit.
  • The observed mid-transit time (2458881.795 BJD_TDB) differs from the prior T0 (2455226.0308 BJD) by ~3655.765 days, which is consistent with ~1055 orbital periods of HAT-P-30 b (period = 3.46845 days). This confirms the correct orbital period was used.
  • The fitted Rp/R* = 0.151 ± 0.045 is larger than the published value of ~0.124, but the uncertainties overlap within ~1σ.
  • The fitted inclination (83.41° ± 2.48°) differs from the prior (86.4° ± 0.5°) but remains within ~2σ.

Uncertainties: The large uncertainty on Rp/R* (±0.045, ~30%) and the inability to constrain transit duration indicate that this single-night observation provides a moderate-quality constraint on the transit parameters. A full transit coverage observation would significantly improve these measurements.

Conclusions

This observation of HAT-P-30 b using the LCOGT 0.4m telescope at Tenerife successfully detected the transit signal of this hot Jupiter. The fitted mid-transit time is T_c = 2458881.795 ± 0.017 BJD_TDB, consistent with ephemeris predictions based on the known orbital period. The radius ratio estimate of Rp/R* = 0.151 ± 0.045 is consistent with the published value (Rp/R* ≈ 0.124) within uncertainties, though the large error bars reflect the limitations of a partial transit coverage with a small-aperture telescope.

The data quality is reasonable for this instrument class, with per-point uncertainties of ~1.35% and residual scatter of 1.12% after airmass detrending. The observation would benefit from:

  1. Full transit coverage (ingress to egress)
  2. More frequent observations (shorter cadence)
  3. Calibration frames (flats, darks, biases)

This dataset is suitable for AAVSO submission and contributes to the citizen science monitoring of HAT-P-30 b.
