Scientific Report: Transit Observation of WASP-69 b

Observation Summary

The transit of exoplanet WASP-69 b was observed using the MicroObservatory 0.4-meter telescope (instrument 0m4-92) equipped with a Sony QHY600M CCD detector. The observation was conducted on September 24, 2025, using a Johnson V filter. A total of 300 FITS images were acquired with an individual exposure time of 12.0 seconds.

Data Reduction

Data reduction was performed using the EXOTIC (EXOplanet Transit Interpretation Code) pipeline. The images were pre-processed with bias, dark, and flat-field calibrations as specified in the FITS headers. Aperture photometry was conducted, identifying the target star at pixel coordinates [1203, 1220] and a set of comparison stars at [427, 1308], [2388, 1691], and [1555, 809]. The best-performing comparison star was identified at [427, 1308]. The optimal aperture size was determined to be 4.27 pixels with an optimal annulus of 10.23 pixels. Detrending was applied to the light curve using airmass coefficients (a1 = 1.008 +/- 0.004, a2 = -0.007 +/- 0.024) to account for atmospheric extinction.

Transit Results

The light curve was fitted using a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach via UltraNest. The resulting planetary parameters are as follows:

  • Mid-Transit Time: 2460939.8849 +/- 0.0063 BJD_TDB
  • Radius Ratio (Rp/Rs): 0.111 +/- 0.048
  • Transit Depth: 1.24 +/- 1.07 %
  • Orbital Inclination: 84.52 +/- 2.32 degrees
  • Transit Duration: 0.0 +/- 0.0 days

Data Quality and Discussion

The photometric precision of the dataset was high, with a residual scatter of 0.4% in the light curve fit. However, the transit duration was not constrained (0.0 +/- 0.0 days), and the uncertainty in the transit depth (1.07%) is nearly as large as the depth itself (1.24%). These results suggest that while the photometric noise was low, a clear transit signal was not strongly detected or well-captured within this specific observation window, leading to poor constraints on the duration and depth.
