ExoAgent Reports for Expert-Based Evaluation

EXOTIC successfully completed the reduction of 300 CCD FITS images of WASP-69 b obtained with the Exoplanet Watch 0.4-meter telescope on 2025-09-24. The data were provided as uncalibrated science frames; no Flats, Darks, or Biases were present, so no calibration correction was applied. The images were processed with aperture photometry, alignment, comparison-star normalization, airmass detrending, and EXOTIC’s transit-model fitting. A clear/open passband was represented using a 350–850 nm custom wavelength interval.

The extracted sequence has an approximately 15-second cadence and covers about 1.25 hours. Frame-level diagnostics show no alignment, FITS, low-flux, out-of-frame, or sky-background flags in the displayed plate-status entries. However, EXOTIC did not identify a best comparison star from the supplied ensemble. This is a significant data-quality limitation because comparison-star normalization is required to distinguish a transit from atmospheric and instrumental variations.

The formal EXOTIC fit returned a mid-transit time of BJD-TDB 2460939.8822 ± 0.006, radius ratio Rp/Rs = 0.061 ± 0.047, and depth 0.37 ± 0.57%. It returned a zero-duration transit and residual scatter of 0.42%. No signal-to-noise ratio, reduced chi-squared, or BIC was reported. The fitted depth is statistically consistent with zero and is also inconsistent with a well-constrained WASP-69 b transit, whose catalog radius-ratio prior is approximately 0.131. In addition, the fitted epoch falls well outside the actual observing interval, which spans approximately BJD 2460943.554–2460943.606.

Therefore, this reduction should not be interpreted as a positive or negative precision transit measurement. The scientifically supported conclusion is that the dataset does not constrain the WASP-69 b transit. The likely causes are an unsuitable comparison-star selection and/or an observing window that did not cover the expected transit sufficiently. Reprocessing with visually verified comparison stars and an observation centered on the predicted transit window is recommended.
