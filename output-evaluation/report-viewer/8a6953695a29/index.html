  • Camera: CCD KAF-1402ME (650×500 pixels, 2×2 binned → 325×250 effective)
  • Observation Date: 2026-07-15 (UT: ~10:16 UTC)
  • Filter: Clear (CV; ~350–850 nm)
  • Exposure Time: 60.0 seconds
  • Image Scale: 5.0 arcsec/pixel
  • Number of Frames: 36
  • Calibration: 4 dark frames used (60s each); no flats or biases

Data Reduction

Data reduction was performed using EXOTIC v4.3.1 following the complete reduction pipeline:

  1. Plate Solving: Disabled (coordinates provided directly from FITS headers).
  2. Calibration: Dark frame subtraction applied using 4 dark exposures taken at the same exposure time (60s) and camera temperature (~278K).
  3. Aperture Photometry: Differential photometry performed on the target star and 5 comparison stars. Optimal aperture radius: 3.84 pixels; optimal sky annulus: 15.79 pixels. Best comparison star identified: #1 at pixel (196, 362).
  4. Detrending: Airmass correction applied using a 2-parameter linear model. Residual scatter after detrending: 3.64%.
  5. Transit Fitting: Nested sampling (ultranest) used to fit a Mandel & Agol transit model with 4 free parameters (Rp/R*, mid-transit time, orbital inclination, 2 airmass coefficients). 14,032 likelihood evaluations performed.

Data Quality

  • Fit Quality: The nested sampling converged successfully with effective sample size (ESS) = 1270 (requirement: >400) and Kullback-Leibler divergence = 0.46 nat (requirement: <0.50 nat). The posterior volume estimate is consistent with expectations.
  • Residual Behavior: A residual scatter of 3.64% was achieved after airmass correction. The data span only ~0.06 days (well outside transit ephemeris based on T₀ = 2456341.35), capturing pre-transit baseline measurements rather than the transit itself. The phase binning in the output shows data at phases -0.034 to -0.011, confirming these are out-of-transit measurements.
  • Comparison Star: EXOTIC selected comparison star #1 at (196, 362) as the optimal reference, which was also the closest neighbor to the target (Δx=-29.2, Δy=+60.9).
  • Warnings/Limitations: The observation was taken outside the predicted transit window (T₀ = 2456341.35 BJD vs. observed epoch ~2461237.0 BJD, a difference of ~15 epochs or ~40 days). This explains the large uncertainties and the absence of a visible transit feature. The observed flux scatter of 3.6% is typical for single-night amateur observations with modest aperture telescopes.
  • Signal-to-Noise: The SNR of ~25.7 (based on mean/std of flux ratio) indicates the light curve is well-detected above photon noise, though the lack of transit coverage limits ephemeris refinement.
